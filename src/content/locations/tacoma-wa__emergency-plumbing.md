## Why Tacoma Homes Face Emergency Plumbing Failures

A lot of Tacoma's housing was built in the early-to-mid 1900s, and that era of construction leaned on galvanized steel and cast iron piping that's now well past its intended service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the diameter until pressure spikes find the weak point, usually at a joint or an elbow buried behind plaster or under a crawlspace. Tacoma's hillier neighborhoods also mean a lot of homes sit on sloped lots with crawlspace foundations rather than slab, and those crawlspaces are often under-insulated. During the occasional hard freeze that rolls in off the Sound, exposed supply lines in those unconditioned spaces are the first to split. Add in Pierce County's heavy winter rain and clay-heavy soil, which holds water against foundations longer than sandier ground would, and a slow leak under a house can go undetected for weeks before it surfaces as a soaked carpet or a spike in the water bill.

## Our Emergency Plumbing Process in Tacoma

We start every call by isolating the source, whether that means the main shutoff, a fixture valve, or in older homes, tracking down a shutoff that was relocated or buried during a past remodel. From there we assess what the water touched: subfloor, insulation, wiring, and in pre-1960s homes, sometimes original plaster that behaves differently than modern drywall once it's saturated. We document everything with photos before any demolition, which matters when a claim goes to an adjuster who wasn't on-site. Repairs follow local plumbing code requirements enforced through Pierce County and City of Tacoma permitting, and any section of pipe we replace is brought up to current code rather than patched to match the old system. Drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with moisture readings taken at the start and logged until materials hit dry standard, not just dry to the touch.
