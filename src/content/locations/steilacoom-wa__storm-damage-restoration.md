## Why Steilacoom Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Steilacoom sits directly on Puget Sound, and that exposure cuts both ways: the water view comes with unobstructed wind during fall and winter frontal systems, and homes along the bluff take the brunt of it. Mature evergreens, the same firs and cedars that give the town its character, come down or shed large limbs during sustained gusts, and root systems loosened by saturated bluff soil are a recurring cause of fence, roof, and siding damage after a multi-day rain event. Older homes near the historic downtown core often still have original roofing details and single-pane window assemblies that were never built for sideways-driven rain, so wind-pressure intrusion at the roofline and window flashing shows up more here than in newer Pierce County subdivisions.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Steilacoom

We start with a roof and envelope assessment to find where wind or falling debris broke the building's water barrier, then move to emergency tarping or board-up to stop ongoing intrusion before extraction begins. Standing water gets pulled with truck-mount extraction, and we set containment around any plaster or original lath we find in pre-war construction, since those materials hold moisture longer than modern drywall and will re-wet a dried room if they're rushed. Our crews carry IICRC Water Restoration and Structural Drying training, and we document every affected material with moisture readings so the drying plan, and the insurance claim, has a clear paper trail. Once structural moisture content is back to a stable baseline, we scope reconstruction, matching siding, trim, and roofing profiles where the home's age makes an exact match harder to source.

## Local note

Homes built along Steilacoom's bluff edge deal with a slower-moving version of storm damage: heavy rain saturates the slope, and that saturation can back up foundation drainage and crawlspace vents for days after the storm itself has cleared. We check grading and crawlspace moisture on bluff-adjacent properties as a standard part of the job, even when the visible damage is limited to roof or siding, because a foundation that stays damp through the following week is where mold problems start.
