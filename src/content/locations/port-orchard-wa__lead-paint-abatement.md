**Dealing with chipping or peeling paint in an older Port Orchard home?** If your house went up before 1978, that paint may contain lead, and disturbing it during a renovation, a water loss repair, or routine maintenance can turn a simple project into a health hazard for your family or tenants. Frontline Fire & Flood handles lead paint testing and abatement across Kitsap County under EPA Lead-Safe Certified practices, so the work gets done without spreading contaminated dust through the rest of the house.

## Why Port Orchard Homes Face Lead Paint Risk

Port Orchard's older housing stock, much of it clustered in the neighborhoods near downtown and along the Sinclair Inlet waterfront, was largely built before the federal lead paint ban took effect. Add in the Puget Sound region's wet, marine climate, and you get a slow but steady cycle of moisture intrusion, paint failure, and flaking that exposes older coats underneath. We see it most often on exterior trim, window sashes, and porches facing the water, where wind-driven rain and salt air accelerate paint breakdown faster than it would in a drier inland climate. Combine that with the renovation activity that comes with Kitsap County's steady population growth, and a lot of contractors end up disturbing lead paint without realizing it.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Port Orchard

Every job starts with testing, not assumptions. We take paint chip or XRF samples from suspect surfaces, confirm lead content, and document the results before any containment goes up. From there, the work follows EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) protocols: the work area gets sealed off with plastic sheeting, HVAC vents are isolated, and crews use HEPA-filtered vacuums and wet-sanding methods instead of dry sanding or open-flame paint removal, both of which can aerosolize lead dust. Debris is bagged as hazardous waste and disposed of per state requirements. Once the abatement is complete, we run a final HEPA vacuum pass and visual clearance check before releasing the space back for painting or reconstruction. If your Port Orchard property also needs repair work afterward, like reconstruction, interior remodeling, or water damage repair from the same source that exposed the old paint, we can scope that as a follow-on job rather than bringing in a second contractor.

## Reaching Port Orchard from Lakewood

We run crews out to Port Orchard regularly from our Lakewood base, heading north along Highway 16 toward the Tacoma Narrows Bridge and across into Kitsap County. Because lead abatement work is scheduled rather than same-hour emergency response, we coordinate arrival windows directly with homeowners and contractors so containment can go up and testing can happen before any other trade touches the surface. That matters in older homes where multiple subcontractors might otherwise be working in the same space, since lead containment has to be sealed off before drywall, flooring, or paint crews start.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Port Orchard specifically: a lot of the pre-1978 homes near the waterfront and older downtown blocks have had multiple paint jobs layered over the decades, sometimes five or six coats deep. That layering means a surface that tests negative on a quick swab can still have lead-bearing paint two or three coats down, which only shows up once sanding or scraping starts. We test at the depth the project will actually disturb, not just the top coat, which avoids a surprise mid-project stop-work.
