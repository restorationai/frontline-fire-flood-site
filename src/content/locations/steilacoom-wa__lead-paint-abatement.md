Lead paint abatement in Steilacoom comes with a wrinkle that doesn't apply in newer Pierce County suburbs: this is the oldest incorporated town in Washington, and a meaningful share of its housing stock predates the 1978 federal lead paint ban by decades, not just a few years. Add in the marine layer moisture rolling off Puget Sound that keeps old exterior paint perpetually damp and chalking, and you get friable lead-based paint showing up on window sashes, porch rails, and clapboard siding well before a homeowner expects it.

## Why Steilacoom Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Homes built before the mid-20th century in Steilacoom were often finished with multiple coats of lead-based paint, and many have never been fully stripped or encapsulated. The town's proximity to the water means higher ambient humidity and more freeze-thaw cycling on painted wood trim than you'd see further inland. That cycling cracks and chalks old paint faster, releasing lead dust into soil beds, window wells, and gutters. Renovation or demolition work on these structures, even something as routine as replacing a window or sanding a porch rail, can disturb decades-old paint layers and trigger EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) requirements that a lot of contractors aren't set up to handle correctly.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Steilacoom

We start with lead paint testing, either XRF analysis on-site or paint chip sampling sent to a lab, to confirm what we're dealing with before any containment goes up. Once lead is confirmed, we seal the work area with plastic sheeting and negative air pressure to keep dust from migrating into living space or outdoors, which matters more on older Steilacoom lots where homes sit close together. Removal methods depend on the substrate: wet scraping and chemical stripping for trim and siding, full component removal for badly deteriorated window sashes or doors. HEPA vacuuming and wet-wipe cleaning follow every phase, and we close out with clearance testing before we call a space lead-safe again. Debris is bagged, labeled, and disposed of per EPA hazardous waste handling rules, not just hauled to a regular dumpster.

## Local note

One thing we watch for on Steilacoom jobs that doesn't come up as often elsewhere: a lot of the town's historic homes have painted exterior trim that was never meant to be stripped down to bare wood, because the original wood species (often old-growth fir) was selected assuming it would always be coated. Full paint removal on these surfaces can expose wood that wasn't finished for UV or moisture exposure, so on exterior work we talk through encapsulation as an alternative to full strip-out wherever the substrate allows it. It's a cheaper, faster, and often more appropriate fix for a porch rail or window trim that isn't actively failing.
