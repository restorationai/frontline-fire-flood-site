## Why North Puyallup Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of North Puyallup was built during periods when crawlspace foundations, rather than full basements, were the regional standard. Those crawlspaces sit close to a water table that rises noticeably during the wet season, which runs roughly October through April here. When a pipe bursts or a roof leak goes undetected, moisture doesn't just pool on the surface, it wicks into subfloor insulation and framing members that are already fighting ambient ground moisture. Add in clay-dense soil that drains slowly after storms, and you get a property that stays saturated longer than a comparable home in a well-drained area. Older homes with galvanized plumbing, still common in parts of the city, add another failure point: internal corrosion that leads to pinhole leaks inside walls, often undetected until flooring starts to cup or drywall starts to bubble.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in North Puyallup

We start every call with a moisture assessment, not a guess. That means thermal imaging to trace water migration behind walls and under flooring, followed by extraction with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on access. Because so many North Puyallup properties have crawlspace foundations, we pay close attention to subfloor insulation batts, which hold water long after the surface reads dry, and vapor barriers that may need to be pulled and replaced rather than dried in place. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers based on the actual psychrometric readings in the space, not a flat formula, and we re-check moisture content daily until the materials hit dry standard. For finished basements or slab-on-grade additions, we monitor subfloor moisture separately since concrete can trap water that never shows up on a surface meter.
