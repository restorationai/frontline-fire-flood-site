## Why Bremerton Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

A lot of Bremerton's housing stock went up fast during the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard's wartime expansion, and plenty of those homes still carry original galvanized or cast iron supply and drain lines. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the channel until a weak spot finally gives, often at a joint behind a wall or under a slab where nobody sees it coming. Add Kitsap County's wet winters and the freeze-thaw swings that hit exposed crawlspace plumbing, and you get a steady stream of burst-pipe calls from November through February. Homes closer to the water table, especially those downhill toward the waterfront and ferry terminal area, also deal with slower drainage, which means a plumbing failure sits and saturates subfloor longer before anyone notices the smell or the soft spot in the flooring.

## Our Emergency Plumbing Process in Bremerton

First call is always about stopping the water: locating the shutoff, isolating the failed section, and confirming nothing upstream is still feeding the leak. From there we assess what the water touched, because a burst supply line in a crawlspace behaves differently than a failed drain stack inside a finished wall. In older Bremerton homes with plaster-over-lath construction, water tracks sideways along the lath before it ever shows on the surface, so we check moisture readings well past the visible wet line. Once the active leak is handled, we move into extraction and structural drying per IICRC S500 guidelines, documenting moisture content at each stage so the repair and the insurance paperwork match up.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of out-of-area crews off guard: many Bremerton homes built before the 1960s have mixed plumbing generations, meaning a single house might have galvanized supply lines feeding a newer PEX addition from a kitchen or bath remodel.
