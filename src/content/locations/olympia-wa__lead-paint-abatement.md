**Lead paint abatement in Olympia?** If you're renovating a home built before 1978, state and federal rules mean the work has to be handled by EPA Lead-Safe Certified crews, not a general contractor with a sander. Olympia's older neighborhoods, especially the bungalows and foursquares built up around the capitol campus in the early-to-mid 1900s, carry layers of lead-based paint under decades of repainting. Frontline Fire & Flood handles testing, containment, and removal so your renovation stays compliant and your household stays safe.

## Why Olympia Homes Often Test Positive for Lead Paint

Olympia's housing stock skews older than a lot of Western Washington suburbs. The city grew up around state government well before the 1978 federal lead paint ban, and a large share of homes in and around downtown, the eastside, and the older streets near Capitol Lake date to that pre-ban era. Add in the Pacific Northwest's wet winters, and you get a second problem layered on top of the first: moisture intrusion causes paint to blister, crack, and chalk faster than it would in a drier climate, which releases lead dust into gutters, window wells, and soil around the foundation. Window sashes and exterior trim, the parts of a house that take the most weather exposure, are almost always where we find the highest concentrations during testing.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Olympia

We start with EPA-compliant testing, either XRF analysis or lab-submitted paint chip samples, to confirm which surfaces are positive before any work begins. Once we have results, we seal the work area with plastic sheeting and negative air pressure to keep dust from migrating into living space or outdoors, which matters here given how often renovation work in Olympia happens during the rainy months when windows and doors stay closed. Removal follows EPA RRP protocols: wet-sanding or chemical stripping instead of dry sanding, HEPA-filtered vacuums for cleanup, and double-bagged disposal of contaminated debris as hazardous waste. We finish with a clearance check before we call the area safe to reoccupy.

## Olympia Permitting and Renovation Coordination

Because Thurston County and the city both expect lead-safe work practices documented on pre-1978 renovation permits, we coordinate our abatement scope with whatever reconstruction or remodeling work follows. If your project also involves drywall, trim replacement, or window work, we sequence the abatement first so the finish crew isn't walking contaminated dust back into a clean space. For rental properties and multi-unit buildings common near the Capitol Campus and downtown core, we also document the work for landlords who need records on file for tenant notification requirements.

## Local note

Olympia's high humidity and frequent freeze-thaw cycles in winter cause exterior lead paint to fail faster on north-facing walls and under eaves, areas that stay damp longest after a storm front moves through. When we test older homes in town, those shaded, moisture-exposed surfaces consistently come back with higher lead concentrations than south-facing walls that get more sun exposure and dry out between rains. If you're only testing part of a house to save money, those are the surfaces worth prioritizing.
