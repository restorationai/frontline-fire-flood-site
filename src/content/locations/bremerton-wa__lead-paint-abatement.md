**Lead paint abatement in Bremerton** comes with a specific wrinkle: much of the city's housing was built during the shipyard-driven boom years of the 1940s, when lead-based paint was standard on both interior trim and exterior siding. Add in the Kitsap Peninsula's wet, marine climate, and that paint doesn't just age, it chalks, peels, and sheds dust faster than it would in a drier inland market. If you're renovating, selling, or just worried about peeling trim around a window a toddler can reach, the work needs to be done to EPA RRP standards, not guessed at.

## Why Bremerton's Housing Stock Raises Lead Paint Risk

Bremerton grew rapidly around the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard, and a large share of its single-family homes and duplexes date to that WWII and immediate postwar era, before the 1978 federal ban on residential lead-based paint. Combine that construction timeline with Kitsap County's rain and humidity, and painted exterior trim, window sashes, and porch rails take a beating. Moisture gets under old paint layers, causes them to lift and flake, and turns a stable painted surface into friable dust and chips around sills, thresholds, and anywhere wood meets wood. That's the exposure pathway regulators care about most, and it's usually worse on the weather-facing side of a house, something a visual inspection from the street won't tell you.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Bremerton

We start with EPA Lead-Safe Certified testing of the specific surfaces in question rather than assuming an entire house is contaminated. If lead is confirmed, work areas get sealed with poly containment, HEPA-filtered negative air where needed, and wet methods throughout to keep dust from migrating into living space or outside onto soil near garden beds and play areas. Paint and substrate are removed or stabilized depending on condition and your renovation plans, debris is bagged and disposed of per hazardous waste rules, and the area gets a final HEPA vacuum and wet wipe clearance before containment comes down. Because older Bremerton homes often have lead paint layered under newer coats, we test at the layer being disturbed, not just the top coat, which is where DIY sanding jobs usually go wrong.

## Bremerton Permitting and Disclosure Considerations

Lead-based paint disclosure is a federal requirement on pre-1978 homes at the point of sale or lease, and Kitsap County and City of Bremerton permitting can come into play when abatement is tied to a larger renovation or demolition project. We coordinate the abatement scope with your contractor or listing timeline so the work doesn't stall a closing date or hold up a permit inspection waiting on clearance paperwork. If you're working with a real estate agent or a buyer's inspector, we can provide documentation of the testing and clearance results so there's a clean paper trail.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Bremerton specifically: a lot of the pre-1950 housing stock has exterior siding repainted multiple times over the decades, sometimes with lead paint sandwiched between newer latex coats. A surface scrape can look clean while the layer underneath, the one that gets disturbed during window replacement or siding repair, is the actual hazard. We test at the disturbance depth, not just the visible surface, because that's where most under-the-radar exposure happens on older Kitsap Peninsula homes.
