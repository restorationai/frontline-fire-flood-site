## Why Milton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Milton's low elevation relative to the Hylebos Creek watershed means stormwater and groundwater both sit closer to the surface than in neighboring hill towns like Edgewood. A lot of the housing stock here, especially the mid-century single-level homes near the historic downtown core, was built on vented crawlspace foundations rather than poured slabs. Those crawlspaces are efficient at venting summer humidity but terrible at shedding a sudden water event: a cracked supply line or a washing machine hose failure can pool under the subfloor for days before anyone notices the smell or the soft spot in the hallway. Add in Western Washington's wet season, roughly October through March, and soil around foundations stays saturated long enough that even a minor roof or gutter failure finds its way into wall cavities.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Milton

We start by identifying the source and category of water, because a line break reads very differently than floodwater carrying creek sediment. For Milton's crawlspace-heavy housing stock, that means getting a technician under the house early, not just inspecting from inside, to confirm whether insulation batts and floor joists are saturated. Extraction comes first with truck-mount or portable units depending on access, then we set containment and begin structural drying per the IICRC S500 standard, which accounts for how long framing lumber and subfloor sheathing actually need to release moisture rather than just read dry at the surface. Moisture mapping continues daily until readings match the home's dry standard, not a fixed number of days.
