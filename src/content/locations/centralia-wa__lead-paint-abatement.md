**Lead paint abatement in Centralia often starts with a simple home renovation that turns up a problem nobody expected.** Centralia's older neighborhoods, many built during the city's early 1900s timber and coal boom, still carry layers of lead-based paint under decades of subsequent coats. When a window gets replaced, a porch gets rebuilt, or a wall gets sanded for repainting, that old paint becomes airborne dust, and that's a different kind of hazard than the fire or water damage most people call us about.

## Why Centralia Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Federal law banned lead-based residential paint in 1978, but a large share of Centralia's housing stock predates that by decades. The city's historic core grew up around the timber and rail industry in the early 1900s, and many of those homes have been repainted multiple times without the old layers ever being properly removed. Add in the Pacific Northwest's wet, mild climate, which keeps exterior paint cycling through expansion, moisture absorption, and peeling faster than it would in a drier region, and you get a steady supply of chipping, chalking paint on siding, trim, and window sills exactly where kids and pets spend time outside.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Centralia

We start with testing, because guessing at which layer of paint contains lead is how renovation projects turn into containment failures. Once we confirm lead presence, we seal off the work area with physical containment and negative air pressure, following EPA RRP work practices so dust and chips don't migrate into living spaces or outside soil. Removal is done with HEPA-filtered tools rather than dry sanding or open-flame paint stripping, both of which are prohibited under the lead-safe rules for a reason: they're the fastest way to spread contamination. After abatement, we HEPA vacuum and wet-wipe every surface in the containment zone and verify clearance before we call the area safe to reopen.

## Lewis County Permitting and Older Homes

Renovation work on pre-1978 homes in Lewis County is subject to the same EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule that applies statewide, and that rule doesn't bend for a quick weekend project. If a contractor is disturbing more than a small amount of painted surface in a home built before 1978, EPA-certified renovator practices are required, not optional. We handle that certification piece as part of the job so homeowners and landlords aren't left trying to interpret federal rules on their own, and so the paperwork trail exists if a tenant, buyer, or inspector ever asks for it later.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Centralia specifically: a lot of the siding and trim on homes from the early timber-boom era was repainted with oil-based paints well into the 1960s and 70s, which means a single wall can have several generations of lead-containing coats stacked under newer latex paint. Surface testing that only samples the top layer can miss that older material entirely, so we test at multiple depths rather than assuming the most recent coat tells the whole story.
