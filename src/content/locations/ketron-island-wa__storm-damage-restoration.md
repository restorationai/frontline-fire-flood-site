## Why Ketron Island Properties See Storm Damage

Ketron Island sits exposed in Puget Sound with dense stands of Douglas fir and cedar crowding most lots. When a winter windstorm rolls through, those same trees that give the island its privacy become the biggest threat to roofs, siding, and detached structures. Limbs and whole trees come down on power lines and rooflines alike, and because many homes here rely on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal utilities, a downed line or a flooded drain field can turn a storm event into a multi-system repair. Wind-driven rain also finds its way through roof penetrations and older trim details common on the island's cabin-style and mid-century homes, so water intrusion often shows up days after the wind itself has died down.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Ketron Island

We start with safety and containment: securing broken windows, tarping compromised roof sections, and board-up for any opening that's letting weather or wildlife into the structure. From there we assess moisture intrusion using the same IICRC S500 drying standard we apply across Pierce County, which matters more on an island property because a missed pocket of wet insulation or subfloor can't be addressed on a whim the way it could be on the mainland. Debris and downed limbs get cleared from roof and gutter systems before we move into structural drying, and if a well or septic component was affected by flooding or a fallen tree, we coordinate that scope separately so it doesn't get buried inside the general repair estimate.

## Reaching Ketron Island from Lakewood

There's no vehicle bridge to Ketron Island, only the small Pierce County passenger ferry out of Steilacoom, so getting restoration equipment on-site takes more coordination than a typical call in Lakewood or University Place. We work out ferry schedules and, when the job requires heavier equipment like air movers or a generator, arrange separate transport so the crew isn't waiting at the dock with gear that won't fit on a passenger run. Property owners who call ahead of a known storm system give us the best shot at having materials staged and ready before conditions worsen.

## Local note

Because Ketron Island homes are almost universally on private wells, a storm that knocks out power for an extended stretch often means the well pump is down too, which can mask a slow leak or a saturated crawlspace until the power comes back and someone notices standing water. We check well-pump status and crawlspace moisture as a standard part of storm assessments here, even when the visible damage is limited to the roofline, because the electrical side of island living changes how storm damage actually reveals itself.
