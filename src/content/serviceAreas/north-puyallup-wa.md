## Restoration emergencies common in North Puyallup

North Puyallup sits near the Puyallup River valley, an area shaped by snowmelt runoff from Mount Rainier and the seasonal flooding that has defined the valley for generations. Heavy Pacific storm systems and atmospheric river events between November and March push saturated ground past capacity, and homes with crawlspace foundations are often the first to show it: standing water under the subfloor, insulation sagging with moisture, and framing that stays wet long after the rain stops. Older galvanized and cast-iron plumbing common in the area's postwar housing stock adds a second failure point, with pinhole leaks and slow drain backups that go unnoticed inside a wall cavity for weeks. Between the valley's flood history and the region's year-round humidity, mold colonization after any undetected leak moves fast, often within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in North Puyallup

Much of North Puyallup's housing dates from the postwar decades through the 1980s, with crawlspace foundations dominant on the valley's lower ground and a mix of slab-on-grade and daylight basements on the higher terrain. Those crawlspaces are the first place we check on any water call, since moisture trapped under the subfloor can sit unnoticed for weeks in a home with poor venting.

Plumbing vintage tracks the construction era: galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains in the older stock, copper through the 70s and 80s, and PEX in more recent remodels or new builds. Older homes also carry the usual era-linked hazards, asbestos in floor tile, siding, and pipe wrap, and lead paint on pre-1978 trim and siding, both of which shape how a restoration scope is sequenced.

Because North Puyallup sits in unincorporated Pierce County rather than inside Puyallup's city limits, permits for structural repair or rebuild generally go through Pierce County's Planning and Public Works department rather than the City of Puyallup's building division. The valley's flood history also means some parcels fall under floodplain management rules that can affect how a rebuild is scoped and permitted.
