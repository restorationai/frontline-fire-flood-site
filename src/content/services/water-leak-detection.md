A water bill that's climbed for no obvious reason, a warm patch on the slab, a faint hiss behind drywall when the house goes quiet at night: these are the signals that send homeowners searching for water leak detection in Lakewood before any visible damage shows up. By the time water stains the ceiling or baseboards start to swell, the leak has usually been running for weeks. Finding it early, without tearing out walls or flooring to guess, is the entire point of this service.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Leak detection is a diagnostic service, not a demolition job. Our technicians use acoustic listening equipment to pick up the sound of pressurized water moving through pipe, thermal imaging cameras to spot temperature differences in walls and slabs caused by moisture, and penetrating moisture meters to confirm what the other tools suggest. For a suspected slab leak, we may also run a pressure isolation test on the plumbing system to narrow the leak to a specific line before anyone touches the concrete. Most non-invasive scans take one to two hours; confirming a hidden leak in a wall or under a slab, with access point placement, can take longer depending on the home's plumbing layout and how many fixtures are tied into the suspect line.

## Our process

1. **Symptom review.** We start with what you've noticed: a spike in the water bill, a sound of running water with everything off, warm or damp flooring, musty odor in one room.
2. **Non-invasive scanning.** Acoustic and thermal equipment map likely leak zones without opening walls or cutting flooring.
3. **Pinpoint confirmation.** Moisture meters and, where needed, pressure testing narrow the source to a specific pipe run, fitting, or slab section.
4. **Minimally invasive access.** If the leak needs to be physically verified, we open the smallest access point possible, usually a few inches, rather than a full wall or floor section.
5. **Documentation and handoff.** You get a written report with moisture readings, thermal images, and the confirmed leak location to give to your plumber or insurance adjuster.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake in this trade is guessing. A plumber or handyman without detection equipment will often open the wall or ceiling closest to the stain, which may be nowhere near the actual source since water travels along framing, insulation, and subfloor before it surfaces. That means unnecessary drywall removal, a leak that's still active, and a second round of cuts once the real source is found.

A second common gap is incomplete documentation. Insurance adjusters evaluating a water leak in wall claim want to see moisture mapping, not just a verbal description of where the leak was. We log readings at the point of origin and at the boundary of the affected material, so there's a clear record of how far the water traveled, which matters for both the repair scope and the claim. Skipping this step is one of the fastest ways a claim gets questioned or underpaid.

Finally, a good detection visit checks for secondary damage, not just the leak itself. A slab leak running for months can saturate soil under the foundation and wick moisture into baseboards and drywall well beyond where the pipe actually failed. If that saturation isn't flagged at detection, it gets missed until mold or structural issues show up later.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Lakewood and the surrounding South Puget Sound area have a mix of mid-century homes on slab foundations and newer construction, and both come with their own leak patterns. Older cast iron and galvanized supply lines common in homes built before the 1980s are prone to pinhole corrosion, which often shows up first as a slab leak rather than a visible pipe failure. The region's clay-heavy soil also shifts with our wet winters, putting stress on buried supply and drain lines and making slab leak detection more common after a particularly rainy stretch. Homes with exposed plumbing in crawlspaces or unheated additions are also worth checking after a hard freeze, since a cracked line may not leak visibly until temperatures rise and the ice inside melts.
