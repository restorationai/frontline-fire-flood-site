**Burst pipe emergency in Lakewood?** We answer 24/7, call (253) 200-0503 now. A supply line that lets go behind a wall or under a slab can put out hundreds of gallons an hour, and the damage compounds every minute water keeps moving through drywall, subfloor, and insulation.

A burst pipe rarely announces itself politely. Sometimes it's a copper line that split overnight during a cold snap and you wake up to water pooling under the bathroom door. Sometimes it's a slow pinhole leak behind a shower wall that finally gives way, or a slab leak under the kitchen that shows up as a warm spot on the floor and a water bill that doesn't make sense. Each of these needs a different combination of water extraction, pipe repair coordination, and structural drying, and the order you do things in matters.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

The first hour is about stopping the source and pulling standing water before it migrates further into wall cavities, cabinetry, and flooring seams. We isolate the water supply (or confirm it's already been shut off), extract standing water with truck-mount or portable extraction equipment, and start assessing how far the water traveled using moisture meters and, where needed, thermal imaging. Pipe material matters here: copper, PEX, and galvanized lines fail differently, and a line that burst once under pressure can hint at a larger issue with corrosion or freeze exposure elsewhere in the system.

Once the active leak is addressed, the job shifts to drying. Wet drywall, baseboards, and subfloor don't always look wet from the surface, which is why moisture mapping drives equipment placement rather than guesswork. Drying typically runs several days with air movers and dehumidifiers positioned and monitored daily, documented against an IICRC S500 drying standard so there's a clear record of when the structure actually returned to a normal moisture range.

## Our process

1. **Source control** - confirm the water supply is shut off and identify where the pipe failed (wall cavity, slab, ceiling, under a fixture).
2. **Extraction** - remove standing water from flooring, cabinetry toe-kicks, and any low points before it wicks further.
3. **Moisture mapping** - use meters and thermal imaging to trace how far water traveled behind walls and under flooring, not just where it's visible.
4. **Controlled drying** - place air movers and dehumidifiers based on the mapped wet zones, with daily moisture readings logged until materials are back in range.
5. **Repair coordination** - open, patch, and if needed coordinate with a licensed plumber on the actual pipe repair, then close up drywall, trim, and flooring once drying is confirmed complete.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake is drying the visible surface and calling it done while water is still sitting inside a wall cavity or under a subfloor. Drywall can read dry to the touch while the insulation behind it stays saturated for weeks, which is exactly the setup for hidden mold growth. A good response pulls baseboards or drills small inspection holes to verify what's happening inside the wall rather than guessing from surface readings.

Another gap is treating every burst pipe as the same event. A clean supply-line break is a very different cleanup than a drain line or any water that's been sitting for more than 24 to 48 hours, which starts shifting into a different water category requiring more aggressive containment and disposal of porous materials. Adjusters look closely at documentation here: moisture logs, photos of the affected cavity before and after opening it up, and equipment run-time records are what support a claim instead of a flat verbal description of "it got wet and we dried it."

## What does burst pipe cleanup and repair cost?

Costs vary with how much water escaped, how long it ran before discovery, and whether it traveled into a second floor, wall cavities, or a finished basement. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote; every loss is different, and Frontline Fire & Flood provides a written scope before any work begins.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, though coverage for the plumbing repair itself versus the water damage cleanup can be split between different parts of a policy, so it's worth confirming with your carrier early.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Lakewood's winters bring enough freeze-thaw swing that uninsulated pipe runs in crawlspaces, garages, and exterior walls are a recurring source of burst pipe calls, especially after a hard overnight freeze followed by a quick thaw. Older homes in the area with galvanized supply lines are also more prone to pinhole failures as the pipe corrodes from the inside, which can look like a sudden burst but is really years of wear reaching a breaking point.
