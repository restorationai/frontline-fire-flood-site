## When Do You Need a Contents Restoration Call Instead of a Plumber or a Painter?

You need a contents specialist whenever smoke, soot, standing water or sewage has touched furniture, fabrics, electronics or paper goods, not just the walls around them. A plumber stops the water source. A contractor rebuilds the structure. Neither one is equipped to safely clean, dry or store what was inside the room.

This matters most after house fires in older Tacoma neighborhoods like the North Slope Historic District or Hilltop, where homes often have dense, fabric-heavy interiors that hold smoke odor for weeks if nobody treats them with the right equipment. It also matters after a burst pipe or storm-driven water intrusion near the Stadium District or along the Thea Foss waterway corridor, where finished basements and lower-level rec rooms take the brunt of a flood.

If the damage is contained to one item, a soaked rug, one scorched chair, you can often handle cleaning yourself. Once multiple rooms or a whole floor of contents are affected, professional pack-out and cleaning protects far more of what you own.

## What Happens During a Pack-Out, From Pickup to Return?

A contents pack-out moves your belongings through four stages: inventory, removal, cleaning or restoration, and either storage or return. Each stage is documented in writing so nothing gets lost in the process or in the insurance file.

1. **Inventory and photo documentation.** Every item leaving the property is logged, often by room, with photos taken before it's touched. This list becomes the backbone of the insurance claim.
2. **Pack and transport.** Items are wrapped and boxed by category (soft goods, electronics, documents, furniture) and moved to a dedicated cleaning facility, not stored loose in a truck.
3. **Cleaning and restoration.** Soot, smoke odor or water contamination is treated with the method appropriate to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, specialized laundering for textiles, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor, electronics assessment for anything with circuitry.
4. **Storage or return.** Items that can go home right away are returned once the structure is dry and cleared. Items that need to wait out repairs go into climate-controlled storage until the space is ready.

## What Belongings Can Actually Be Saved After a Fire or Flood?

Most hard goods and many soft goods can be saved if they're treated within days, not weeks, of the damage. Solid wood furniture, metal, glass, and most electronics respond well to professional cleaning. Delay is what turns a salvageable item into a loss.

Paper documents and photographs are the most time-sensitive category: water-soaked paper starts to degrade and mold can begin forming within [24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) on wet material, so these need freezing or specialized drying fast. Upholstered furniture, carpeting, and mattresses are a case-by-case call: Category 1 (clean) water on a couch cushion is often recoverable, but Category 3 water, from a sewer backup or storm floodwater, usually means porous items like upholstery and padding get discarded rather than cleaned, per [ANSI/IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) guidance. Smoke-damaged electronics should be inspected before being powered back on; soot is conductive and can short a circuit board.

## How Does Contents Restoration Fit Into an Insurance Claim?

Contents restoration strengthens an insurance claim because it replaces guesswork with a documented, itemized inventory. Most homeowners policies cover personal property damaged by a sudden, covered loss like a fire or a burst pipe, but the payout depends heavily on what you can prove you owned and its condition before the loss.

An itemized pack-out list, backed by before-and-after photos, gives the adjuster something concrete to work from instead of a reconstructed memory of what was in the closet. It also separates cleanable items (a reimbursable cleaning cost) from total losses (a replacement-cost claim), which matters because those are paid differently under most policies. Washington homeowners can review their coverage basics through the [Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/).
