## How Much Does Mold Remediation Cost in Tacoma, WA?

Most Tacoma homeowners pay between $500 and $8,000 for mold remediation, with whole-house or HVAC-distributed contamination running higher. The number depends almost entirely on how much area is affected and where in the house it's growing.

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Small bathroom or closet patch (under 10 sq ft, surface mold only) | $500 - $1,500 |
| Bedroom, hallway, or single wall section (10-50 sq ft) | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Attic sheathing and insulation | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Crawl space mold (common under older Tacoma homes) | $3,000 - $8,000 |
| Whole-house or HVAC-distributed contamination | $8,000 - $15,000+ |
| Pre- and post-remediation clearance testing | $300 - $900 |

## What Changes the Price?

Three things move a mold remediation estimate more than anything else: square footage affected, how accessible the area is, and how long the mold has been growing undetected. A job doubles in cost fast when mold has spread behind walls or through an HVAC system instead of sitting on one visible surface.

Square footage is the most obvious driver, but accessibility often matters more. A patch of mold on an open bathroom wall is quick to contain and remove. The same square footage inside a cramped crawl space under a 1940s North Tacoma craftsman, with low clearance and no easy access point, takes longer and costs more because the crew is working in tighter conditions with more protective setup.

Hidden mold also changes the math. Surface mold you can see and measure is cheap to scope accurately. Mold that's spread inside wall cavities, under flooring, or along ductwork isn't fully known until containment is opened up, which is why some estimates include a contingency range rather than a flat number.

Here's how that plays out by location:

- **Small bathroom or closet patch**: contained space, limited materials, often resolved in a single day. Lowest cost tier.
- **Attic sheathing**: requires removing and replacing insulation plus treating roof decking, with careful handling around trusses and vents.
- **Crawl space**: common in Tacoma's older housing stock, where vapor barriers are thin or missing and Puget Sound humidity keeps ground moisture high. Limited access and the need for full-suit PPE push labor time up.

## Why the Moisture Source Has to Be Fixed First

Mold remediation that doesn't fix the moisture source isn't a fix, it's a delay. Mold can begin growing on wet material within 24 to 48 hours according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home), so as long as the leak, condensation, or ventilation problem continues, the mold comes back.

A legitimate estimate separates remediation (removing and treating affected material) from repair (fixing the plumbing leak, grading issue, or ventilation gap that caused it). Sometimes one contractor handles both; sometimes remediation and a [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) crew coordinate on the source repair while a separate line item covers the mold itself. Either way, ask directly: does this estimate include fixing what caused the moisture, or only cleaning up after it?

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold in Washington?

Most standard Washington homeowners policies cover mold only when it results from a sudden, accidental covered loss, like a burst supply line, and even then coverage is often capped at a specific dollar limit. A slow leak, long-term condensation, or a maintenance issue that caused the mold is typically excluded.

That distinction matters because it's the first thing an adjuster checks. If a pipe burst overnight and mold shows up a week later during drying, that's usually treated differently than mold found behind a shower surround that's been leaking for months. Washington's [Office of the Insurance Commissioner](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/) and the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) both note that mold coverage under a standard policy is limited and policy-specific, so the actual language in your declarations page is what decides it, not a general rule. Photograph the damage as soon as you find it and get a written scope before work starts; that paper trail is what supports a claim either way.

## What Happens During Mold Remediation, and Is Testing Worth It?

A typical mold remediation job in Tacoma runs through five phases: inspection, containment, removal, cleaning, and clearance. Each phase protects the rest of the house from cross-contamination while the affected material is treated.

1. **Inspection**: the crew identifies the extent of visible growth and checks for moisture behind the surface with a meter.
2. **Containment**: poly sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the work area so spores don't spread to the rest of the home, following the [IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/).
3. **Removal**: porous materials that can't be cleaned (drywall, insulation, some flooring) are removed and bagged for disposal.
4. **Cleaning and treatment**: remaining surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an antimicrobial.
5. **Clearance**: a final check, sometimes with third-party air or surface sampling, confirms the area is back within normal levels before containment comes down.

Testing is worth paying for in two situations: before remediation, when you need to know whether a stain is actually mold or just discoloration (our [guide to testing for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/) covers DIY kits versus a lab-verified sample), and after remediation, when you want third-party confirmation the job is actually done, not just a visual check from the same crew that did the work.

## How to Read a Mold Remediation Estimate

A solid estimate tells you the square footage being treated, not just a flat total. It should also separate labor, containment setup, disposal and material removal, antimicrobial treatment, and testing into distinct line items, so you can see what you're actually paying for.

Watch for estimates that skip the moisture-source question entirely, bundle "testing" without saying whether it's visual or lab-verified, or give a single number with no square footage attached. Those gaps usually mean surprises once the wall comes open.
