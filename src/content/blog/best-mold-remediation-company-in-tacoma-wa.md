## What Does IICRC AMRT Certification Actually Mean?

IICRC AMRT certification means a technician has completed formal training in microbial remediation science and follows [ANSI/IICRC S520](https://iicrc.org/s520/), the industry standard for mold remediation. That standard covers containment barriers, negative air pressure, PPE requirements, and the documentation a clearance inspection should include.

The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org/) is the certifying body that trains and tests technicians on this standard. A company that references AMRT without being able to name the certification, or that can't explain containment levels when you ask, likely hasn't actually sent anyone through the course. It's a fair, specific question to ask directly: "Which of your technicians holds AMRT certification?"

## How Do You Tell a Real Remediation Job From a Bleach-and-Paint Fix?

A real remediation job isolates the contaminated area, removes affected porous materials, and HEPA-filters the air before anyone repaints anything. A bleach-and-paint job just hides the stain and leaves the spores and the moisture source behind.

Here's what a proper job generally includes, per the S520 standard:

- **Containment**: poly sheeting sealed around the work area so spores don't spread to the rest of the home during removal
- **Negative air pressure**: an air scrubber running HEPA filtration, pulling air out of the containment zone rather than pushing contaminated air into clean rooms
- **Removal of affected porous materials**: drywall, insulation, and carpet that mold has colonized usually can't be cleaned and dried in place; it gets cut out and bagged
- **Source correction**: finding and fixing the actual moisture problem, whether that's a bathroom fan venting into the attic instead of outside, a crawl space vapor barrier that's failed, or a roof leak
- **Post-remediation clearance**: a verification step, sometimes by a third-party testing company, confirming spore counts in the air have returned to a normal range before the job is considered done

That last point is worth asking about directly. A number of reputable firms use an independent testing company for clearance, specifically so the company that did the removal isn't also the one grading its own work. If a company offers to "test and clear" the job itself with no independent verification option, ask why, and what documentation you'll get at the end.

## What Questions Should You Ask Before You Hire?

Ask these before any company sets foot in your Tacoma home:

1. Are your technicians IICRC AMRT certified, and can I see the certification?
2. Will the job include containment and HEPA filtration, or just surface cleaning?
3. Will you identify and document the moisture source, not just the visible mold?
4. Is post-remediation clearance testing included, and is it done by an independent party?
5. Will I get a written scope of work before anything starts?

A company confident in its process will answer all five without hesitation. One that gets vague, or tries to redirect the conversation toward urgency, is telling you something.

## What Are the Red Flags to Avoid?

The biggest red flag is whole-house fogging sold as a standalone fix, with no containment, no source correction, and no clearance testing behind it. Fogging has a narrow, legitimate use as part of a documented process. It is not a substitute for removing contaminated material or fixing the humidity problem that caused the growth.

The second red flag is a fear-based sales pitch, the inspector who tells you your family is in danger and you need to sign today, before explaining what the actual scope of work is or giving you a written estimate. Mold exposure is a real health consideration worth taking seriously, and the [EPA's guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) is a good independent reference point. But urgency should come from a documented inspection, not a pressure tactic.

A Tacoma crawl space or attic with recurring moisture almost never resolves with one visit and a bottle of bleach. If you're seeing that pattern, it's worth getting a second, written opinion before any work starts.
