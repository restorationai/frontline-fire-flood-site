## What Should You Check Before Hiring a Water Damage Company in Tacoma?

Check three things before any company starts work: an active Washington contractor license, IICRC certification for the technicians actually on site, and whether they'll document moisture readings for your insurance claim. A company that skips any of these leaves you exposed if the adjuster questions the scope later.

License status is public record. Washington's [Department of Labor and Industries verification tool](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) lets you confirm a contractor license number in under a minute, which is worth doing before a crew starts cutting drywall. IICRC certification matters because the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) is the industry benchmark for how water damage should be categorized, dried, and documented, not just mopped up.

Insurance coverage is the other piece. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water loss, like a burst supply line, but not gradual leaks or unendorsed sewer backups, as covered in our [guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/). A restoration company that photographs the loss and logs daily drying readings gives the adjuster what they need to approve the claim faster.

### How do I verify a water damage company is actually licensed in Washington?

Use the Washington State Department of Labor and Industries' [contractor verification tool](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) and search the company's license number. Any licensed contractor's status, bond, and insurance standing show up immediately, and a company that resists giving you its license number is a red flag.

### Does my homeowners insurance cover a company's water damage repairs?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or failed appliance line, but exclude gradual leaks and most sewer or sump pump backups unless you have a water backup endorsement. Document everything with photos and keep your restoration company's drying logs, since insurers rely on that paperwork to approve the claim.

### What's the difference between IICRC certification and a general contractor license?

An IICRC certification trains technicians in how to categorize water, dry structures to the S500 standard, and prevent secondary mold growth. A contractor license is a state-issued credential confirming the business is legally allowed to perform construction and repair work in Washington. A reputable water damage company should hold both.
