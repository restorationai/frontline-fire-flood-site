## What Should You Look For When Comparing Fire Damage Restoration Companies in Tacoma?

The strongest fire restoration companies share four traits: IICRC certification specific to fire and smoke, a current Washington contractor license, true 24/7 crew availability, and hands-on experience documenting a loss for an insurance adjuster. Price matters less than whether the company can prove all four.

Fire damage is not the same skill set as water damage, even though many companies advertise both. [IICRC](https://iicrc.org/) FSRT training covers how soot behaves on different materials, how smoke odor migrates into wall cavities and HVAC systems, and which cleaning agents actually neutralize it instead of masking it. A crew without that training can leave a house looking clean while the smell returns within weeks.

Licensing matters for a different reason: it confirms the company is accountable to the state. You can [verify any Washington contractor's license](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) directly through the Department of Labor and Industries before you sign anything. A company that hesitates when you ask for its license number is telling you something.

## What Does a Fire Restoration Company Actually Do?

A fire restoration company stabilizes the structure, removes smoke and soot residue, and neutralizes odor before any rebuilding happens. The work generally runs in this order:

1. **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure broken windows, a burned-through roof section, or exposed walls against weather and intrusion.
2. **Water removal**, since most structure fires are put out with hoses, leaving soaked drywall and flooring behind the soot.
3. **Soot and smoke residue cleaning** on walls, ceilings, and hard contents, using techniques matched to the surface (dry sponging for some surfaces, wet cleaning for others).
4. **Odor removal**, often with HEPA air scrubbers or thermal fogging to reach smoke particles that have settled into carpet, drywall, and HVAC ductwork.
5. **Contents restoration and storage**, pulling salvageable belongings offsite to clean and store them while the structure dries and clears.

## What Questions Should You Ask Before Hiring a Fire Restoration Company?

Ask for the company's IICRC certification type, its Washington license number, and whether it will provide a written scope of work before starting. A company confident in its credentials will answer all three without hesitation.

Beyond that, ask who handles the insurance adjuster conversation, whether contents will be cleaned on-site or moved offsite, and what the estimated timeline looks like for your specific damage class. Minor kitchen fires with light smoke spread can wrap in a week or two; a structure fire with major smoke migration into multiple rooms can run several weeks, especially if reconstruction trades are waiting on an insurance decision.

One more question worth asking: does the company distinguish between fire damage and smoke damage in its scope? They're related but not identical.
