**TL;DR:** Fire damage restoration in Tacoma, WA typically costs anywhere from a few hundred dollars for smoke-odor-only cleanup to $40,000 or more for a structural fire that destroys multiple rooms. The biggest cost drivers are how much structure burned versus how much just absorbed smoke, whether contents need pack-out and cleaning, and how fast board-up and drying started after the fire department left. Most homeowners insurance policies cover fire damage, but the final bill depends on the written scope, not a flat per-square-foot number.

If the fire department just cleared your Tacoma home and you're staring at a scorched kitchen or a smoke-stained living room, you probably have two questions: what is this going to cost, and what will insurance actually pay. There's no single number, because a contained grease fire in a North End craftsman and a whole-floor fire near the Stadium District get priced completely differently. What follows is how restoration companies actually build that number, so you can read an estimate and know whether it makes sense.

## How much does fire damage restoration cost in Tacoma, WA?

Most fire damage restoration jobs in the Tacoma area fall somewhere between a few hundred dollars for light smoke cleanup and well over $40,000 for a structural fire that requires rebuilding. The total is built scenario by scenario, not from a single flat rate, because fire jobs combine several different kinds of work: cleaning, demolition, odor treatment, and sometimes reconstruction.

Here's how the typical ranges break down by scenario, based on standard industry pricing patterns for residential fire loss:

| Scenario | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Smoke odor only, no visible structural damage (burnt food, small appliance fire) | $500 to $3,000 |
| Contained fire, one room, smoke residue on adjacent rooms | $3,000 to $10,000 |
| Structural fire, one to two rooms, charred framing or drywall | $10,000 to $25,000 |
| Multi-room or whole-house structural fire | $25,000 to $50,000+ |
| Contents cleaning and pack-out (per room, separate from structure) | $500 to $3,000 per room |
| Debris removal and demolition (charred materials, damaged flooring) | $1,000 to $8,000 depending on volume |

These are typical industry figures, not a quote. Every fire is different, and a written scope of work after an on-site inspection is the only number that actually applies to your property.

## What drives the price up or down?

The price moves based on how much of the damage is structural versus surface-level, and how quickly mitigation started. Four factors matter most: the extent of the burn, the type of smoke, contents volume, and response time.

**Extent of the burn.** A fire that stayed contained to a stove or a single wall costs far less than one that compromised framing, subfloor, or roof structure. Structural char means demolition and rebuild, which is a different cost category than cleaning.

**Type of smoke.** Not all soot is equal. Protein fires (burnt food) leave a thin, hard-to-see residue that's actually difficult to clean. Synthetic-material fires (furniture, electronics, plastics) leave a thick, oily soot that penetrates porous surfaces like drywall and insulation, often requiring those materials to be removed rather than cleaned, per [ANSI/IICRC S700](https://iicrc.org/s700/), the industry standard for fire and smoke restoration.

**Contents volume.** A fire in a furnished home with a full kitchen, closets, and electronics costs more to restore than a sparsely furnished rental, because contents cleaning and pack-out storage are billed separately from structural work.

**Response time.** Soot becomes acidic and etches metal, glass, and finishes within days. A property that sits unboarded and untreated for a week after the fire department leaves will usually cost more to restore than one where board-up, tarping, and HEPA air scrubbing started within 24 to 48 hours. That's also why [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) typically happens before the full damage assessment: it stops the cost clock from running up due to weather and secondary soot migration.

## What does homeowners insurance typically cover for fire damage?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies cover fire damage, including the structure, smoke damage to unburned areas, and personal belongings, subject to your policy limits and deductible. Fire is one of the named perils covered by nearly every standard policy, unlike flood, which requires separate coverage.

What gets disputed isn't usually whether fire is covered, it's the scope: whether smoke-damaged drywall needs to be removed or can be cleaned, whether contents need professional cleaning versus replacement, and whether code-required upgrades (like new smoke detectors or updated wiring exposed during repair) are included. A detailed, itemized scope of work with photo documentation is what gets those line items approved rather than argued over. Washington homeowners can check their policy terms and file complaints through the [Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/) if a claim isn't being handled fairly.

## What does the restoration process actually involve, and why does it affect cost?

Fire restoration happens in a sequence, and each phase is its own cost center on the estimate. Skipping or rushing any step usually costs more later, not less.

1. **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure broken windows, damaged roof sections, and openings before weather or vandalism adds damage.
2. **Water extraction**, if firefighting efforts left standing water, which is common and often overlooked in the cost conversation.
3. **Soot and debris removal**, separating what can be cleaned from what has to be demolished.
4. **Structural cleaning and deodorization**, including HEPA vacuuming, thermal fogging, or ozone treatment for embedded smoke odor (see our [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) process for how persistent smoke smell gets treated versus masked).
5. **Contents cleaning and pack-out**, moving salvageable items off-site for ultrasonic or specialty cleaning.
6. **Reconstruction**, rebuilding anything that was demolished: drywall, flooring, cabinetry, paint.
